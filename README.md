# Cardano Incident Catalogue
This repo contains a public collection of incidents that happened on Cardano or within Dapps on Cardano, with the goal to provide transparency to companies wanting to build on Cardano. 
Incidents could be technical or organisational.  

## Structure

Each incident is in its own directory, named CID-yyyy-xxx - Cardano Incident Disclosures - where `yyyy` is the year the incident occurred, and `xxx` is a number, sequenced by when the incident was added to this repo. 


The `xxx` sequence may create confusion about an implied timeline within a given year, but there is no complete list of incidents today, this repo is open for contributors, and should be open for disclosures made months after they happened and got resolved before reporting about them for security reasons.

### Why provide a history of incidents transparently - arent incidents bad? 
**No**

In the end, Cardano is just a tech platform, a tech provider, similar to how AWS would be one. Decision makers that choose the underlying tech for realising their ideas do not require a perfect history, they require to know what happened in the past, how it got handled and what changed in order to avoid a similar incident in the future. Traditionally, in Web-2 tech service companies, this is addressed at scale with ISAE-3000/SOC-2 audits and certifications, which govern how a company treats and controls certain risks pertaining to their technology. When a company passes the annual audit, their clients can tick off pages of their own due diligence checklists in one go, saving costs, and reducing risks, which makes the audited company more attractive. The point of these audits is not to not have risks, the point is to handle issues well when they occur (not if they occur). 
In Web-3, there is no equivalent SOC-2-style audit for decentralised tech providers (albeit attempts were made), so addressing tech provider risks is still reflected in manual due diligence forms of large institutions looking for tech. In these forms questions about incidents are made with the goal to learn about the management of the risks of the chain (like a SOC-2 audit), not to learn about technical details of a singular incident. Brushing them under a carpet, or hiding them in secret councils isn’t helping, because no incidents can only mean the tech is too young to have experienced one, which subsquently means the network is not ready for when an issue inadvertadly arises, or no one is using the tech. Even more so: an open incident catalogue is not about admitting defeat(s), it’s about proving Cardano is battle tested and means it survives the next unavoidable incident. 


Knowing how Cardano broke in the past, is very valuable for development - especially in the light of AI-assisted development.
